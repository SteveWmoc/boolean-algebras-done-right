# Boolean Algebras Done Right

An algebra-first Lean 4 development of Boolean algebras, their homomorphisms, representations, and infinitary theory.

The guiding principle is simple:

> Boolean algebras are treated as algebraic structures; their order and lattice structure are derived.

The project takes the Boolean signature

- two binary operations,
- one unary complement operation,
- two distinguished constants,

as foundational. Order-theoretic structure is then proved from the Boolean identities rather than assumed at the definition site. In particular, Boolean homomorphisms are intended to be defined intrinsically as homomorphisms of this algebraic signature.

The long-term development is informed by classical sources including Halmos, Sikorski, and the *Handbook of Boolean Algebras*, while remaining driven by formalization needs arising in Boolean-valued mathematics.

## Initial plan

The first milestone is deliberately small:

1. choose and formalize a clean equational axiom system for Boolean algebras;
2. derive the elementary identities;
3. derive the canonical partial order and bounded distributive lattice structure;
4. define intrinsic Boolean homomorphisms and prove their basic API;
5. verify the design on algebras of sets;
6. provide explicit interoperability with Mathlib where useful.

The internal roadmap is a living document. The project does not aim to formalize every theorem in the classical literature before applications can move forward.

## Status

The primitive Boolean signature and equational axioms, elementary identities,
derived order, intrinsic homomorphisms and isomorphisms, and the category of
Boolean algebras are implemented. Concrete examples include power set,
trivial, and two-element algebras.

`BooleanAlgebra.Subalgebra A` describes substructures as subsets closed under
meet, join, complement, zero, and one. Each subalgebra inherits a Boolean
algebra structure on its carrier subtype. `S.subtype : BooleanAlgebra.Hom S A`
is the canonical injective inclusion, with simplification lemmas for all five
primitive operations.

`SetExamples.SetAlgebra X` specializes subalgebras to the power set algebra
of `X`. `IsSetAlgebra X F` states the concrete family conditions: every member
is a subset of `X`, the family contains the empty set and `X`, and it is closed
under binary intersection, binary union, and relative complement in `X`.
`SetAlgebra.equivFamilies X` proves the two presentations equivalent. Each
`SetAlgebra X` inherits its Boolean algebra and inclusion homomorphism from
the general subalgebra API.

`SetExamples.FiniteCofiniteAlgebra X` is the Boolean algebra of subsets `s ⊆ X`
for which `s` or `X \\ s` is finite. Its closure under the Boolean operations
is proved through `IsSetAlgebra`, so its Boolean identities are inherited.
For finite `X`, the finite-cofinite family equals the full power set.

For infinite `X`, `SetExamples.finiteCofinite_cardinality` proves that this
algebra has cardinality `Cardinal.mk X`. The construction yields
`BooleanAlgebra.exists_of_infinite_cardinality`: every infinite cardinal
is realized by a Boolean algebra.

For any topological space `X`, `SetExamples.ClopenAlgebra X` is the Boolean
algebra of clopen subsets, constructed as a power set subalgebra.
`clopenEquiv X` identifies its carrier with the subtype of clopen sets;
the operation lemmas identify meet, join, and complement with intersection,
union, and set complement. No separation or compactness assumptions are needed.

For a linear order `X`, `SetExamples.IntervalAlgebra X` is the Boolean algebra
generated by the strict initial segments `Set.Iio a`. It contains final rays
`Set.Ici a` and half-open intervals `Set.Ico a b`. The least-algebra property
and finite endpoint dependence are proved. No density or endpoint assumptions
are required.

`LogicExamples.LindenbaumAlgebra T` is the classical propositional algebra of
formulas modulo biconditionals provable from an arbitrary theory `T`.
Derivability is inductive (theory axioms, classical tautologies, modus ponens);
provable equivalence is an equivalence relation and a congruence for the
Boolean connectives. The quotient is a Boolean algebra, its truth class is
exactly the provable formulas, and it is trivial exactly when falsity is
provable.

`FirstOrderLogic.FormulaAlgebra T` extends this to Mathlib first-order
formulas with natural-number free variables and any theory of sentences.
The Hilbert calculus includes capture-avoiding universal instantiation,
universal distribution, vacuous generalization, equality reflexivity and
substitution, modus ponens, and universal generalization. Boolean laws follow
by lifting propositional derivations. Universal quantification also descends
to the quotient. Class equality is provable biconditional equivalence, truth
is provability, and inconsistency is exactly triviality. No semantic-entailment
rule or completeness theorem is assumed; full first-order soundness and
completeness theorems are not part of this construction.

`FirstOrderLogic.variableSubalgebra T V` contains the formula classes admitting
a representative with free variables in the finite set `V`. Its carrier
`VariableAlgebra T V` inherits a Boolean algebra and the injective inclusion.
Membership is equivalently provable biconditional equivalence to such a
representative; it does not require every representative to have restricted
free variables. The construction is monotone in `V`, and every class belongs
to one of these finite-variable subalgebras.

`FirstOrderLogic.LindenbaumAlgebra T` is reserved for the sentence quotient.
`ClosedFormula L` represents sentences as formulas with no free variables
in the existing natural-number variable syntax. `LindenbaumAlgebra.closedSubalgebraIso T`
is a Boolean algebra isomorphism with `VariableAlgebra T ∅`, and
`LindenbaumAlgebra.inclusion T` embeds it into the larger `FormulaAlgebra T`.
Its image is exactly the empty-variable subalgebra. Class equality is provable
biconditional equivalence; the truth and inconsistency characterizations hold
for the sentence algebra as well.

`Dual A` exchanges meet with join and zero with one while retaining complement.
Instantiate an existing theorem on `Dual A`, then use the `Dual.ofDual_*`
simplification lemmas to obtain its dual. `Dual.doubleDualIso A` identifies the
double dual with the original algebra.

`BooleanDuality.Term` and `BooleanDuality.Statement` provide explicit syntax
for the algebra signature and first-order statements, including quantifiers.
Syntactic dualization is involutive. `Statement.eval_dual` relates evaluation
in the dual algebra to evaluation of the dual statement; `Statement.Valid.dual`
proves the duality principle for universal validity. Logical connectives and
quantifiers are retained, as is Boolean complement.

`BooleanAlgebra.le x y` is defined by `join x y = y`, equivalently
`meet x y = x`. Reflexivity, antisymmetry, and transitivity follow from
the algebraic laws. `BooleanAlgebra.partialOrder A` packages this relation
as a `PartialOrder A`; use `letI := BooleanAlgebra.partialOrder A` to install
it locally and use the usual order notation.

Join is the least upper bound and meet is the greatest lower bound for this
order. `join_le_iff` and `le_meet_iff` express their universal properties.
`BooleanAlgebra.lattice A` packages these proofs as a `Lattice A` on the same
derived order, with supremum equal to the original join and infimum equal to
the original meet. Install it locally with `letI := BooleanAlgebra.lattice A`
to use Mathlib's lattice API.

`BooleanAlgebra.distribLattice A` upgrades the derived lattice to a
`DistribLattice A`, using only the meet distributive axiom. For any lattice,
`LatticeTheory.distributive_iff` proves that meet distributing over join is
equivalent to join distributing over meet; neither complements nor bounds
are needed for this equivalence.

The derived distributive lattice is bounded: `zero_le` and `le_one` prove
that zero is least and one greatest. `le_zero_iff` and `one_le_iff` characterize
the extreme comparisons. Install `letI := BooleanAlgebra.distribLattice A`
and then `letI := BooleanAlgebra.boundedOrder A` to use the bounded distributive
lattice API, with `⊥` equal to the original zero and `⊤` equal to the original one.

The derived bounded distributive lattice is complemented.
`BooleanAlgebra.isCompl_compl x` identifies the original Boolean complement
as a lattice complement, and `isCompl_iff_eq_compl` characterizes all lattice
complements, including uniqueness. After installing the derived lattice and
bounds, `letI := BooleanAlgebra.complementedLattice A` supplies Mathlib's
`ComplementedLattice A` API.

`BooleanAlgebra.isomorphic_iff_orderIso` proves that two Boolean algebras
are algebraically isomorphic exactly when their derived orders are order
isomorphic. `Iso.toOrderIso` and `isoOfOrderIso` keep the same underlying map.
Order isomorphisms preserve joins, meets, bounds, and the unique complement;
`orderIso_map_compl` exposes the last step explicitly. The bridge uses only
local instances for the derived structures.
